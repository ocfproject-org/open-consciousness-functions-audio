# How OCF works

## Guided practice, then everyday use

OCF functions give a focused practice a name, a guided learning session and a
short cue. Choose a purpose that matters to you, learn the practice, then
use its taught procedure in daily life. For example, you might explore Focus
before working with study material or Relax when you want to settle down.

### Cues are like human hotkeys

In the guided session, you practice a function and link it to a short phrase. That link is called anchoring. If the cue doesn’t feel anchored yet, listen to the guided session again. Repeat it as needed rather than aiming for a set number of listens. Once the cue works for you, there’s no need to keep replaying the training.

In everyday use, think or say the phrase silently as taught, then give the familiar state time to return. You don’t have to repeat the guided exercise or remember all its steps.

For example, once you’ve learned [Relax](../functions/OCF-FND-004/), use **Plus Relax** as taught, then give yourself a moment to settle. It doesn’t have to happen instantly. Let it happen without checking whether it’s working.

A function may teach a brief way to use its cue or a separate cue to end it. The function guide gives you those details. In some recordings, the quieter rest section repeats teaching you’ve already heard.

[Start Here](../start/#choose-your-path) shows the learning path. Once familiar,
use the [Quick Reference](../quick-reference/) to find a cue or explore
[Function Chains](../function-chains/) for optional examples.

### Use a learned cue before you meditate

Before you press Play or start an unguided meditation, you can use a cue you already know to ease into the session. Try [Plus Relax](../functions/OCF-FND-004/) to start settling, [Plus Focus](../functions/OCF-FND-002/) to bring your attention to the practice, or [Plus Inner Power](../functions/OCF-PRF-002/) to draw on your confidence and resolve.

Pick whichever fits how you want to begin. These are options, not a three-step routine. Use each cue as taught and give it a moment rather than waiting for a particular feeling before you start.

## What the stereo audio does

An OCF recording can combine spoken guidance, background layers, and tones that change as the session moves from settling in to practice and return. The voice tells you what to do. The sound can give you something steady to listen to and mark a transition. [OCF audio pipeline](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/3b9eabba815dfbde685a0bd01ea8560d9cbbb092/docs/AUDIO_PIPELINE.md) · [OCF sound design](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/3b9eabba815dfbde685a0bd01ea8560d9cbbb092/docs/SEMANTIC_DSP.md)

A **binaural beat** can sound like a soft pulse when you hear two slightly different pitches, one in each ear. The tones are separate in the recording; you hear the rhythm when you listen to both together. Use the [stereo check](../listening/#stereo-test) to make sure each ear gets its own channel.

**Amplitude modulation** is different. It’s a rise and fall in the volume of the sound itself, like turning the volume up and down in a steady rhythm. A recording may use either technique or both. [OCF sound design](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/3b9eabba815dfbde685a0bd01ea8560d9cbbb092/docs/SEMANTIC_DSP.md)

The narration tells you when the session moves to a different Focus level. You don’t need to pick out a change in the background tones to follow along.

## What a typical function session looks like

1. **Get comfortable.** Set up your stereo audio and listen to the introduction. The guide tells you what you’ll practice.
2. **Prepare.** Set distractions aside with the Transmutation Box and relax. Depending on the recording or your Smart Play choice, you may then hear Resonant Tuning, Energy Ball, and an affirmation, in that order when present.
3. **Settle into Focus 10.** The narration guides you into a relaxed but attentive part of the session.
4. **Learn the function in Focus 11.** Follow the exercise and learn how to use its cue. The session may repeat the teaching so you can practice it again.
5. **Rest and finish.** Some sessions repeat the instruction more quietly in a Focus 20 rest section. The ending determines whether you’re guided back to wakefulness or invited to stay asleep.

Not every recording uses every step. For a concrete example, open [Relax](../functions/OCF-FND-004/) and read its guide or transcript. The [Energy Ball lesson](../functions/OCF-EXP-007/) is an example with an extended guided exercise. See the [Glossary and FAQ](../glossary/) for definitions and the [OCF session pipeline](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/3b9eabba815dfbde685a0bd01ea8560d9cbbb092/docs/AUDIO_PIPELINE.md) for the source documentation.

## Open to inspection

The project publishes its function resources, production tools and documentation.
You can read the [audio pipeline](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/main/docs/AUDIO_PIPELINE.md),
[background sound index](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/main/docs/BACKGROUND_AUDIO_INDEX.md)
and [sound design model](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/main/docs/SEMANTIC_DSP.md).
Those development documents can move ahead of the released recordings; this
site's guides and downloads remain tied to their published versions.

## Beyond functions

Standalone guided meditations and connected series are planned as additional
ways to explore OCF. They are not listed as available recordings yet. Unlike a
function-learning session, a general meditation need not teach a reusable cue;
its own guidance will explain how to take part.

[Get involved](../get-involved/) to help shape these future paths.
