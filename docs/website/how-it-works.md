# How OCF works

## Guided practice, then everyday use

OCF functions give a focused practice a name, a guided learning session and a
short spoken cue. Choose a purpose that matters to you, learn the practice, then
use its taught procedure in daily life. For example, you might explore Focus
before working with study material or Relax when you want to settle down.

The cue is a learned verbal prompt associated with the practice during learning. Say or think it silently in your mind; you do not need to voice it aloud. Follow the function’s guide for its activation sequence, when to use it, and any steps for ending or changing the practice.

Give yourself time to become familiar with the practice. You may drift off during a relaxed listening session. In function-learning recordings with a quiet rest section, the softer passages repeat the function teaching you have already heard; they do not introduce a separate set of hidden instructions.

After learning a function, use its cue silently in your mind, following that function’s instructions. Allow time for the practice rather than forcing an immediate response. Use it in suitable everyday situations, and return to the learning track when you want to reinforce the wording and sequence until they feel familiar and you are confident using them.

[Start Here](../start/#choose-your-path) shows the learning path. Once familiar,
use the [Quick Reference](../quick-reference/) to find a spoken cue or explore
[Function Chains](../practice-chains/) for optional examples.

## What you hear

OCF recordings combine spoken guidance with a stereo sound background. The
production system keeps narration and the background separate, then aligns
background changes with the spoken session. The guidance provides the exercise;
the sound provides a structured listening environment.

The audio system can use binaural tones, where the left and right ears receive
different tones, and amplitude modulation, where sound rises and falls in a
rhythmic pattern. These describe how the sound is built, not a measurement of
what your brain is doing. The exact layers and transitions depend on the edition.

Use stereo headphones or earbuds to preserve the intended left/right sound.
[Check left and right](../listening/#stereo-test) before a session, and use the
[Listening Guide](../listening/) for setup details.

## Open to inspection

The project publishes its function resources, production tools and documentation.
You can read the [audio pipeline](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/main/docs/AUDIO_PIPELINE.md),
[background sound index](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/main/docs/BACKGROUND_AUDIO_INDEX.md)
and [sound design model](https://github.com/ocfproject-org/open-consciousness-functions/blob/main/docs/SEMANTIC_DSP.md).
Those development documents can move ahead of the released recordings; this
site's guides and downloads remain tied to their published versions.

## Beyond functions

Standalone guided meditations and connected series are planned as additional
ways to explore OCF. They are not listed as available recordings yet. Unlike a
function-learning session, a general meditation need not teach a reusable cue;
its own guidance will explain how to take part.

[Get involved](../get-involved/) to help shape these future paths.
